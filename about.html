Our Story & Culinary Philosophy

Simple Ingredients, Patient Fire.

Olive & Ember Bistro was conceived as a neighbourhood dining room where Mediterranean woodfire technique meets the seasonal rhythms of Western India.

Natural stoneware, linen napkins, and warm timber create an unpretentious setting for daily dining.

How It Began

A Dining Room Built for Conversation

Too often, modern dining asks you to choose between casual cafés with rushed table turns and formal restaurants that feel reserved for once-a-year occasions. We imagined a middle ground: a warm, sunlit bistro where you can drop in on a Tuesday evening for handmade tagliatelle or spend three slow hours on a Sunday afternoon with family.

Every element of the room—from the acoustic lime-plaster walls to the spacing between tables—was planned so that conversation remains at the center of the meal.

Our country sourdough is naturally leavened and fermented for 36 hours before baking on hearth stone.

Kitchen Craft

Respect for the Harvest

Our menu is intentionally concise. Rather than stocking a massive freezer, our kitchen works with small growers around Pune, Nashik, and Mahabaleshwar for heirloom tomatoes, figs, winter roots, and culinary herbs, alongside coastal fishermen for daily line-caught sea bass and prawns.

In the kitchen, we rely on foundational craft: slow-simmered stocks, cold-pressed olive oil, bronze-extruded and hand-rolled pastas, and a white-oak hearth that imparts gentle smoke without overpowering delicate vegetables.

Zero Shortcuts

Stocks, sauces, bread, pasta, and botanical syrups are prepared in-house from scratch.

Thoughtful Sourcing

Single-estate Coorg coffee, artisanal cheeses, and peak-season regional produce.

Fictional Chef Profile — Demonstration Character

Chef Kabir Ranade

Head Chef & Co-Founder (Fictional Concept Bio)

Note: Chef Kabir Ranade is a fictional character created for the Olive & Ember Bistro website prototype.

After spending a decade working in wood-fired kitchens and small regional bakeries across Southern Europe and India, Kabir envisioned a kitchen that pairs classical pasta and hearth roasting with local Indian harvests. His cooking avoids heavy cream and theatrical garnishes in favour of bright acidity, hearth-charred edges, and clean broths.

“When a fig or a tomato is picked at the right week of the season, our job in the kitchen is simply not to get in its way.”
